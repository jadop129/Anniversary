Chapter I

The day everything changed

There are moments in life that split time into a before and an after. August 4, 2024 was that moment for us. It was just an ordinary day on the calendar — nothing circled, nothing planned — and yet somehow, in the quiet way that the most important things tend to happen, the world quietly rearranged itself. You walked in, and something in the air shifted. Something that had been searching without knowing it was searching finally recognized what it had been looking for.

I don't think I could have predicted you. Not the way you laugh at your own jokes half a second before you finish them, not the way your eyes go soft when you're listening to something that matters to you, not the way you manage to make even the most ordinary Tuesday feel like something worth remembering. You arrived in my life the way good things often do — all at once, and absolutely right on time.

"To love is to find in someone else's being the mirror of your own, and to be endlessly glad of what you see reflected back."

Chapter II

What nine months really means

Nine months is not a long time in the grand scope of things. And yet, in love, time is measured differently. It is measured in conversations that stretched past midnight, in the growing ease of silences that didn't need to be filled. It is measured in the first time something made me think immediately of you — a song, a color, a stupid joke — and how that reflex never stopped, only deepened. Nine months is the distance between a stranger and someone who has become the texture of my everyday life.

In these nine months, we have built something real. Not the rushed, paper-thin version of love that burns hot and fast, but the kind that is patient, that makes room, that knows how to stay. We have learned each other's rhythms — when to push and when to give space, when words are needed and when presence is enough. We have argued and apologized and come out understanding each other a little more fully each time. That, I have come to believe, is what love actually is: the willingness to keep choosing someone even after you have seen all the complicated, beautiful, messy truth of who they are.

And I choose you. Today, tomorrow, and in every ordinary Tuesday that comes after this one.

You make my ordinary extraordinary

It is not the grand gestures that I will carry with me. It is the small ones — the way you reach for my hand, the look you give me across a room, the thousand tiny moments that add up to a life I am genuinely grateful for.

Chapter III

Everything I love about us

I love that we are honest with each other — not cruelly, but genuinely, in the way that only happens between people who trust that the truth will not break what they have built. I love that we have inside jokes that would make no sense to anyone else, small private languages that are entirely ours. I love the way we navigate the world together, comparing notes, laughing at the same things, getting quietly passionate about the things we care about.

I love who I am when I am with you. Not a better version of myself in some forced, performative way — but more fully myself. More willing to be seen. Less afraid of taking up space in my own life. You have that effect on me: a kind of quiet courage that comes from knowing there is someone in your corner who genuinely sees you and thinks you are worth seeing.

I love that this is still the beginning. Nine months in, and I am still discovering things about you that surprise me, that delight me, that make me think: yes, there it is, another reason. The reasons keep accumulating, and I don't think they'll ever stop.

Chapter IV

Here is what I promise you

I promise to keep showing up — not perfectly, not always gracefully, but consistently, because that is what I believe love really asks of us. I promise to listen, even when it's hard, even when I'd rather be right. I promise to celebrate you — not just in the big moments, but in the quiet ones too, in the Tuesday afternoons and the unremarkable evenings that are, really, where most of life lives.

I promise to keep learning you. You are not a puzzle to be solved but a person to be known, and that knowing is a gift I don't intend to take lightly. I promise to make room for your growth even when it is unfamiliar to me, and to ask you to make room for mine. I promise to remember, on the hard days, that difficulty is not a sign that something is wrong — sometimes it is a sign that something is real enough to be worth the effort.

And I promise to never stop telling you that I am glad you exist, because I am, every single day, genuinely and completely glad that you exist, and that somehow, out of all the possible paths and coincidences and choices, yours crossed mine.
